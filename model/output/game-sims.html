Reading a game

Each bar is one final margin, and its height is how often the model expects that exact result. The market's number splits them: bars on the left of it are outcomes where the away side covers, bars on the right where the home side does.

Why 3 and 7 stand out

Football scores are built from field goals and touchdowns, so finals pile up on 3 and 7. Over 3,134 games a margin of exactly 3 happened 15% of the time. That is why a spread of 3 pushes so often.

Likely range

Where the final margin lands in 8 of every 10 simulated games. "JAX+21 to BAL+12" means: anything from Jacksonville by 21 to Baltimore by 12 is a normal result; only 1 game in 10 goes past each end. It is a reality check, not a pick: even a 4-point favourite loses or wins big all the time. If the sims are honest, about eight in ten finals land inside it -- the review page counts that every week.

Claims vs honest

Every game shows the side the model leans and two cover chances. "Claims" is the model taking its own projection at face value. "Honest" pulls the projection toward the betting line first, because historically only a small part of the model's disagreement with the line has shown up in the score: picks it claimed at 61% won 51%. A game is a Bet only if it clears the bar on the honest number, and break-even at −110 is 52.4%, so most weeks have few bets or none. The chart and the projection are still the model's own view of the game.

QB rating

The number beside each quarterback: expected points he adds per play, from every pass, sack and run of his career, recent seasons weighted most. Thin records are pulled toward what first-time starters actually produce (about −0.10), so a backup with one bad game is not mistaken for an average starter. Mahomes is about +0.14.
